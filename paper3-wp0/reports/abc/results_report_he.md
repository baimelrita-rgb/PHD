## הצעת נוסח לפסקת התוצאות (באנגלית)

> On the partially observed grid we isolated the physics check with three detectors on identical residuals, topologies, candidate rules, persistence and scoring: the global bad-data test (A), per-channel thresholds k × noise without a physics check (B), and the same thresholds raised, channel by channel, to 1.5 times a physics bound of the benign residual (C). Each variant chose k on calibration data only, for at most 0.5 false-alarm episodes per benign hour, and the choice was frozen before the test day was read. On the recorded test day B needed k = 10 to meet the target and detected 6 of 23 process attacks, while C met it at k = 1.25 and detected 10 of 23, both with one false-alarm episode in 8.0 benign hours; C detected four instances B missed and B none that C missed (exact McNemar p = 0.125). On five injected days sharing one benign base day, C detected 44 of 107 instances with 0 false-alarm episodes in 12.8 benign hours, against 51 of 107 with 33 episodes (2.58 per hour) for B; at equal realised load B detected 36 of 107. The physics check therefore raises only the thresholds of channels whose benign residual can be large, instead of every threshold. The physics bound was validated on sampled, searched and box-corner states: after adding corner states to the remainder estimate, none of 5,760 new benign states fired a rule and one of 208 earlier counterexample states still did, with a score of 1.02; the bound is empirical, not a proof over the whole envelope. On the fully observed grid the library added two detections on the recorded day and none on the injected days, where it raised false-alarm episodes from 0.73–1.07 to 2.2–8.2 per hour.
