## פסקת מסקנה (באנגלית)

> We traced every rule of the 01-Basic library from its source alert episode to its later use, using a replay of the same recording, detectors and chronology with a structured provenance log (22 alert episodes, 9 covered by an existing rule, 10 rules: 8 G, 2 S; `provenance.md` §1–2). Two rules changed an alert decision in a later attack: the grid-invariant rule for topology S1+S2/L4+L11 detected industroyer instance 11 after 5.7 s through line flow 10 (residual 0.031 against a margin of 0.0067), and the rule for S1+S2/L11 with tap −1 detected drift-off instance 23 after 61 s; without either rule, with all other detectors and rules kept, both attacks are missed, and neither rule added a false-alarm episode (§3–4). Both rules were triggered by detector alarms on benign operator actions (a separator movement and a tap step) that moved the grid into a new topology, and both were active 1,100 to 4,400 s before the attacks. The source event determined which topology the rule covers and when it was built; it did not shape the rule's content: rules rebuilt for the same topology without any alert information and evaluated from the same time made identical decisions on every attack and benign record, although up to 44 % of their margins differed (§5). Rules whose content is read from the event itself, the state-change rules built from two industroyer attacks (lines 2 and 3 opened, four injections dropped to zero), never fired again in 26 later opportunities. On the five injected days the library fired in 4 to 9 attacks per day but never changed a decision. The evidence therefore supports a narrower claim than learning policy content from past attacks: alerts on unfamiliar operating states trigger the construction of physics-checked rules for those states, and on one recorded day this changed two of 16 alert decisions.
