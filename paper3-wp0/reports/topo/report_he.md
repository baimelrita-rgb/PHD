## פסקת סיכום באנגלית

> With per-(channel, topology) noise available to both detectors, the physics floor did not change which attacks an operator would see on the recorded day: both B_top and C_top detected 10 of 23 process attacks (Table 3, recorded rows), and no attack was detected by one method only. It did change the alarm load, but not in the direction the calibration predicted: the floor let calibration select k = 1.25 with zero calibration false alarms, which produced 11 false-alarm episodes in 7.97 benign test hours against 1 for B_top at k = 10 (same rows). On the five injected days, which share one recorded background, the floor removed every false alarm (0 episodes in 12.8 benign hours against 42 for B_top) at the cost of 7 of 107 detections (43 against 50; Table 3, injected totals). Six of the seven attacks missed only by C_top were missed because the floor lay above the residual, and none was lost to waiting once both methods were active (Section 3); four of the seven were caught by B_top through one channel whose noise floor was 0.105 sigma, at residuals of 0.23 to 0.69 sigma, a level the physics bound allows in normal operation (Section 4). The comparison isolates the floor only weakly, because no test topology had enough calibration records for a local noise estimate and both methods fell back to per-channel noise; even one hour of records covered the later maxima on only 73 % of channels. The remainder correction removed the last benign-state violation (0 of 208 regression states and 0 of 1,664 new states above the threshold) without losing any detection. The data contain no independent recorded day, so whether these conditions generalise remains open.
